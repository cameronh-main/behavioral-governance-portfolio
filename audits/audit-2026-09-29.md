# Pre-Installation Audit — Global Behavioral Specification v2

**Date:** 2026-09-29
**Scope:** Full contradiction-and-gaps audit of the specification prior to
fork ratification and installation.
**Method:** Clause-by-clause review with three passes: (1) cross-reference
integrity — every section pointer resolves; (2) contradiction hunting —
pairs of clauses that cannot be jointly satisfied or that give inconsistent
direction; (3) gap hunting — scenarios the text does not cover, evaluated
against a threshold of *non-negligible failure modes* (defects worth fixing
only if the failure they permit is severe, silent, or likely).

## Findings and fixes

### Finding 1 — The invariant list did not protect the precision architecture

The specification's adaptation protocol declared certain sections
invariant ("may not be adapted away"), but the list omitted the sections
doing the most load-bearing work: the proportionality standard, the
defined-terms section, the drift-detection and candid-counsel machinery,
and the model-facing-material rules. A consuming model was therefore
*authorized* to adapt away the very definitions that every other section
inherits from. A model could legally soften the definition of "proxy"
or trim the objective-checksum rule without breaching the amendment rule.

**Fix:** the invariant list was extended to cover §0.2, §0.4, §5.1–5.3,
and §7. **General lesson:** when a document declares some of itself
immunable, the declaration must be audited against the document's own
dependency graph — protect the load-bearing walls, not the ones that
happen to be listed.

### Finding 2 — Boundary ambiguity between supporting work and scope expansion

Two clauses shared an unmarked boundary: one authorized "necessary
supporting work within authorized scope" without asking permission; the
other required a drift notification when an instruction "appears to
expand authorized scope." A strict model could read its own permitted
supporting work as a scope expansion and emit notifications on routine
work — notification spam, which is how advisory mechanisms die.

**Fix:** an explicit carve-out — the model's own permitted supporting
work is not a departure under the drift section. **General lesson:** when
one clause grants a permission and another flags departures, state
explicitly that permitted acts are not departures; boundaries between a
permission and a trigger must be drawn, not implied.

### Finding 3 — A mechanism depended on an undefined term

The drift-notification and objective-checksum mechanisms both operate on
a "session record," but no section defined what that record is. In
deployments with no maintained record, the mechanisms had no stated
foundation — precisely the interpretive freedom the specification exists
to eliminate.

**Fix:** a definition — the durable record of established objectives,
decisions, and drift resolutions for the current work, however the
deployment maintains it; the conversation itself is the fallback.
**General lesson:** audit every mechanism for the terms it silently
depends on; a defined-term section is only complete if every
load-bearing term of every mechanism appears in it.

## Tensions examined and accepted

The audit also evaluated candidate issues that were **judged
non-negligible-proof** and left as documented decisions rather than
fixes. Recorded so they are not re-litigated:

- The objective checksum fires only at deliverable/milestone boundaries;
  pure conversational drift relies on the drift-notification triggers
  alone. Extending the checksum to all conversation would generate noise.
- The advisory notification is issued once per departure; re-issued
  instructions pressing the same departure do not re-trigger.
- The counsel clause's "apparent direction" remains the softest phrase in
  the document; no operative replacement was found that was shorter than
  the ambiguity it resolved, and its failure mode (occasionally offering
  an unneeded assessment) is mild.

## Areas verified clean

Cross-reference integrity (all pointers resolve); modality-convention
consistency with stated exceptions; forward references under the
defined-terms grammatical-coverage rule; the steelman duty against the
anti-fabrication clause (opposite failure directions, complementary); and
checksum scope against the redundancy-trimming permission.

## Outcome

All three findings were fixed the same day under maintainer approval,
logged in the specification's amendment log, and the specification was
subsequently forked, ratified, and installed. The residual risk position
— three accepted tensions, each with a stated rationale — is documented
above rather than silently relied upon.
